# We are Changing our Developer Productivity Experiment Design (METR)

How read this time: full post fetched with curl and converted to text and read in full. This pass resolved the sign convention and corrected how METR's conclusion was summarised.

Primary: METR's follow-up to its 2025 RCT.

- New experiment from August 2025: 57 developers, 143 repositories, 800+ tasks; 10 returning from the original study and 47 new; pay $50/hour instead of $150; median 10 years' experience.
- Estimates, sign resolved: the post writes "a speedup of -18%" with a confidence interval "between -38% and +9%" for the returning developers, and "-4%" (CI "-15% and +9%") for new developers. Since the 2025 study is reported as tasks taking "19% longer", a negative number here means less time, that is, a speedup. Both intervals include zero.
- Why METR distrusts it: "we have observed a significant increase in developers choosing not to participate in the study because they do not wish to work without AI, which likely biases downwards our estimate of AI-assisted speedup." Also "30% to 50% of developers told us that they were choosing not to submit some tasks because they did not want to do them without AI"; lower pay; and unreliable time measurement for developers who run several agents at once.
- Conclusion, corrected: METR says the new data give "an unreliable signal of the current productivity effect", that developers are "likely" more sped up now than in early 2025, but that "our data is only very weak evidence for the size of this increase". It calls its central estimate "likely a bad proxy for the real productivity impact", says the estimate is likely a lower bound on the true effect for these developers, and is redesigning the study. The earlier note's phrase that METR "can no longer measure the effect with this design" is a paraphrase; the post says selection effects will "further limit the validity of this study design".
- Bearing on the split: no reliable 2026 measurement from this study; its direction is toward speedup, but METR itself calls the evidence very weak. Developers declining to work without AI is behavioural evidence they value it, not a productivity measurement.
