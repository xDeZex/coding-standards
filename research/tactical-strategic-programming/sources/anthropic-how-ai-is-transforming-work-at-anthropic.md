# How AI is transforming work at Anthropic

How read this time: full page fetched with curl and converted to text; every quote and figure below checked against it. All were correct; this pass added the sample size, the limitations and the "moving target" caveat.

First-party survey and usage study of Anthropic's own engineers. Self-reported, vendor study. Method: August 2025 survey of 132 Anthropic engineers and researchers (convenience and purposive sampling, selection bias acknowledged in its limitations section), 53 interviews, and analysis of 200,000 internal Claude Code transcripts (Feb and Aug 2025).

- Engineers "tend to delegate tasks that are easily verifiable, where they 'can relatively easily sniff-check on correctness', low-stakes (e.g. 'throwaway debug or research code'), or boring". On average people said 44% of Claude-assisted work consisted of tasks they would not have enjoyed doing themselves.
- The page says: "People consistently said they didn't use Claude for tasks involving high-level or strategic thinking, or for design decisions that require organizational context or 'taste.'" Quote: "I usually keep the high-level thinking and design. I delegate anything I can from new feature development to debugging."
- "This is reflected in our survey data, which showed the least productivity gains for design and planning tasks." The authors add that delegation boundaries are a "moving target", "regularly renegotiated as models improve", and that Claude Code usage data show relatively more design/planning use now than six months earlier. The summary also says engineers are "currently keeping most design or 'taste' tasks".
- "More than half said they can 'fully delegate' only between 0-20% of their work"; the page notes respondents interpret "fully delegate" differently.
- "Paradox of supervision": supervising Claude "requires the very coding skills that may atrophy from AI overuse". Some engineers are not worried ("those skills could come back").
- Some engineers describe their role shifting toward "manager of AI agents"; one estimated "70%+" of their work is now reviewing or revising code.
- Bearing on the split: first-party evidence for the split as observed behaviour (humans keep design, delegate implementation), with the basis for delegation being verifiability and boredom, not proven AI quality, and the authors say the boundary is moving.
