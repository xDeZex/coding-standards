# How AI is transforming work at Anthropic

First-party survey and usage study of Anthropic's own engineers. Vendor self-study, self-reported; fetched through a summariser, so figures should be checked.

- Engineers delegate tasks that are easily verifiable, low-stakes, outside their expertise or boring. About 44% of Claude-assisted work was tasks they would not have enjoyed doing.
- They keep high-level work. Quote: "I usually keep the high-level thinking and design. I delegate anything I can from new feature development to debugging."
- Design and planning showed the least productivity gain in the survey data.
- More than half said they can "fully delegate" only 0-20% of their work; the rest needs active collaboration and validation.
- Concern about skill atrophy and a "paradox of supervision": supervising Claude needs the coding skills that overuse may erode.
- Bearing on the split: the strongest first-party practice evidence for the split as observed behaviour (humans keep design, delegate implementation). Note it is delegation by verifiability and boredom, not delegation because AI is proven good at it, and it says supervision needs tactical skill.
