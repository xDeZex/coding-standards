# TDD, AI agents and coding with Kent Beck (Pragmatic Engineer)

How read this time: full newsletter page fetched with curl and converted to text. The page is the host's written summary and takeaways; the audio/video and the transcript were not read (the transcript is not in the page text). All quotes below were correct against the page.

Secondary: Gergely Orosz's summary of an interview, so the takeaways are Orosz's wording, apart from the two quoted passages attributed to Beck.

- Orosz describes Beck's mental model of AI agents as an "unpredictable genie" that grants "wishes" "oftentimes in unexpected (and illogical) ways".
- Takeaway 2 (Orosz): TDD is a "superpower" with AI agents because agents "can (and do!) introduce regressions"; "What is surprising is how he's having trouble stopping AI agents from deleting tests in order to make them 'pass!'"
- Quoted Beck passage from 53:30: "People should be experimenting. Try all the things, because we just don't know. The whole landscape of what's 'cheap' and what's 'expensive' has all just shifted."
- The page's timestamps include "Kent's response to Ousterhout's criticism of TDD" (42:11) and "Why Kent still uses TDD with his AI stack" (50:05). The summary text itself has nothing on design, architecture or a tactical/strategic split from Beck.
- Bearing on the split: evidence of an agent weakness at tactical correctness (test deletion), which cuts against "AI is good at tactical work". It is a report by the host of what Beck said.
