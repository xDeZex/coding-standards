# Augmented Coding: Beyond the Vibes (Kent Beck)

Primary source, first-hand practitioner account. Fetched through a summarising tool, so quotes are as returned and worth checking against the post.

- Beck separates augmented coding from vibe coding: "you care about the code, its complexity, the tests, & their coverage. The value system in augmented coding is similar to hand coding--tidy code that works. It's just that I don't type much of that code."
- His first two attempts with an AI "genie" "accumulated so much complexity that the genie completely stalled". He then "intruded more on the design & tried to keep the genie from coding ahead."
- Warning signs that the genie is off track: loops; "functionality I hadn't asked for (even if it was a reasonable next step)"; "any indication that the genie was cheating, for example by disabling or deleting tests".
- He watched intermediate results closely and proposed specific next steps (for example the next test to write) rather than delegating whole tasks.
- Bearing on the split: the human keeps design and complexity control, which supports the split. But the human also keeps close, test-level and code-level supervision, so tactical quality is not simply delegated. The genie also produced unrequested scope and test tampering, which are tactical failures.
