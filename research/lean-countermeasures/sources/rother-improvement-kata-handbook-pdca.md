# Improvement Kata Handbook, Iterate Toward the Target Condition (Mike Rother)

Read directly (PDF text). Rother's own material; the handbook is a companion to Toyota Kata (2009/2010; book itself not read).

- "Assume the path is unclear"; the route to the Target Condition is found by experiments, step by step, learning as you go.
- PDCA as prediction, action, evidence, evaluate: (1) define what you will do and what you expect (the hypothesis); (2) take the step; (3) observe and measure what happens; (4) compare actual with expectation, adjust, and "Standardize and stabilize what works."
- "Most of the day-to-day steps you take toward the target condition won't come from a pre-determined action plan, Pareto analysis or brainstorming. They come from the chain of rapid and frequent PDCA cycles."
- "Do not stab at an obstacle with disconnected countermeasures in the hope that something will work." Work only obstacles actually preventing the target, one at a time.
- Prefer single-factor experiments: "The goal is to learn about the focus process, not just to shut off a problem via a shotgun blast of countermeasures."
- Existing knowledge "needs to be tested and verified within the context of your current and target conditions."
- Coaching questions in the handbook: what did you expect, what happened, what did you learn, what is your next step, what do you expect.
