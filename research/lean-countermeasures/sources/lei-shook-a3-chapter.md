# Chapter 1, What Is an A3? (excerpt of Managing to Learn, John Shook, LEI)

Read directly (PDF text). The excerpt carries no separate date; the book is 2008 (from memory and search results, not verified in the PDF).

- A3 elements listed: Background, Current Condition, Goal, Analysis, Proposed Countermeasures, Plan, Followup.
- Analysis: "Analyzes the situation and the underlying causes that have created the gap between the current situation and the desired outcome."
- Proposed Countermeasures: "Proposes some corrective actions or countermeasures to address the problem, close the gap, or reach a goal." Template prompt: "How will your recommended countermeasures affect the root cause to achieve the target?"
- Followup: "Creates a followup review/learning process and anticipates remaining issues."
- Question list in the excerpt: What are the root causes of the problem? What are some possible countermeasures? How will you decide which countermeasures to propose? How will you get agreement from everyone concerned? What is your implementation plan (who, what, when, where, how)? How will you know if your countermeasures work? What followup issues can you anticipate? How will you capture and share the learning?
- "Working an A3 leads participants through a cycle of PDCA (plan, do, check, act)": it forces people to "propose working countermeasures designed to achieve the stated goal, gain agreement, and follow up with a process of checking and adjusting for actual results."
- Format is flexible; "most organizations tweak the design".
